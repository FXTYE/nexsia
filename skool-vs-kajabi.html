Our verdict

These two barely compete — they're built on different philosophies. Skool is a community platform that happens to have a classroom; Kajabi is a marketing platform that happens to host communities. If community engagement is genuinely the heart of your business and you don't need email or funnels, Skool is cheaper, simpler, and better at the thing it does.

But most experts who start with "just a community" eventually add a course, then coaching, then email, then a funnel — and suddenly Skool means stitching together five separate tools. If you already know your business needs marketing infrastructure alongside community, Kajabi's higher price buys you a connected system instead of a pile of subscriptions. Start with what your business needs today, not what looks cheaper on the surface.

Common questions

Skool vs Kajabi FAQs

Is Skool or Kajabi better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they solve different problems. Skool is better for community-first businesses; Kajabi is better if you need marketing automation and course depth alongside community. Match the platform to your business model.
Which is cheaper?
Skool's Hobby plan ($9/mo) is far cheaper upfront than Kajabi's Starter (~$71-89/mo). But once you add the email marketing, scheduling and landing page tools Skool doesn't include, the total cost often converges with or exceeds Kajabi's all-in-one price.
Does Kajabi have community features?
Yes — Spaces, Live Rooms and Challenges are included, similar to Skool and Circle. But community is one feature inside a broader marketing platform, not the core product the way it is on Skool.
Does Skool have email marketing?
No — Skool has no built-in email tools at all. If email matters to your business, you'll need a separate tool like Kit or ConvertKit, or consider Kajabi, which includes full email automation natively.
Can I migrate from Skool to Kajabi later?
There's no official one-click migration — moving community content, members and course material between the two platforms takes manual work either direction. Choose based on where your business is headed, not just where it is today.
