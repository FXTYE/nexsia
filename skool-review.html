The quick verdict

Skool is the best platform available for community-first creators in 2026 — the interface is genuinely the cleanest in the space, gamification actually drives engagement rather than feeling gimmicky, and the built-in discovery page brings you members you didn't have to find yourself. Alex Hormozi's backing and the Skool Games competition have made it the default choice for cohort-based and coaching communities.

The trade-off is deliberate simplicity: there's no email marketing, no funnel builder, and no advanced course tooling like quizzes or certificates. If your business needs marketing automation and sales funnels alongside community, Kajabi is the more complete platform — Skool is the pick specifically when community engagement is the product, not an add-on.

Know before you buy

The Hobby vs Pro fee structure, honestly

This is the detail most Skool reviews bury below the fold, and it's genuinely the whole pricing story. Skool has resisted splintering into five tiers with feature gating — there are only two plans, and they include identical features. The entire pricing decision happens at the transaction-fee layer, not the feature layer.

The $90/month gap is really a bet on your revenue

Hobby costs $9/month with a 10% transaction fee on membership payments. Pro costs $99/month with a 2.9% fee. Below roughly $1,300/month in community revenue, Hobby is cheaper overall. Above that, Pro's lower fee saves you more than the $90/month price difference — model your expected revenue before picking a plan, not just the sticker price.

Everything else is identical between the two tiers: unlimited members, courses, videos, live calls, a custom URL, the affiliate program and mobile apps. Skool does not have a permanent free plan — Hobby at $9/month is the entry point, and there's a 14-day free trial to test the platform before committing.

Common questions

Skool FAQs

Is Skool worth $9-99/month in 2026?
Yes, if community engagement is central to your business — coaching, cohorts, or a paid membership. It's a harder sell if you need email marketing or a funnel builder, since Skool deliberately doesn't offer either.
Should I choose Skool Hobby or Pro?
Model your expected monthly community revenue. Below roughly $1,300/month, Hobby ($9/mo, 10% fee) is cheaper overall. Above that, Pro ($99/mo, 2.9% fee) saves you more than the $90/month price gap. Features are identical on both plans.
Does Skool have email marketing?
No — Skool has no built-in email marketing or automation tools. If email is central to your business, you'll need a separate tool like ConvertKit or Kit and connect it manually, or consider Kajabi, which includes email natively.
What is Skool's affiliate program?
Every Skool community owner automatically gets a 40% recurring commission affiliate program — both for referring new Skool customers and, separately, community owners can let their own members earn commission for referring new paid members.
Skool vs Circle — which is better?
Skool is simpler and has stronger built-in gamification; Circle offers more structured community organization (channels, spaces, threads) for larger or more complex communities. See our full comparison.
Is there a free version of Skool?
No permanent free plan — there's a 14-day free trial, after which Hobby starts at $9/month. That's still one of the lowest entry costs in the community-platform category.
