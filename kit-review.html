The quick verdict

Kit remains the strongest email platform built specifically for creators — the free plan alone supports up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, which is genuinely unmatched in this category, and built-in monetization means you can sell digital products without a separate tool.

But the September 2025 rebrand from ConvertKit to Kit came with a real price shock — the Creator plan jumped from $15 to $33/month at the entry tier, a 120% increase that sent creator-community sentiment into a tailspin. If you're under 10,000 subscribers and don't need automation yet, the free plan is genuinely excellent — Kit is the right pick once you need real automation and can absorb the new pricing.

Know before you buy

The September 2025 price hike, honestly

This is the single event that defines Kit's current reputation. Before September 2025, ConvertKit (as it was still called) was the consensus recommendation across creator communities — Reddit, podcasting groups, blogging forums all pointed new users toward it. The rebrand to Kit came with the first price increase in 12 years, and it wasn't small.

Creator jumped 120% — some legacy users saw 100-400% increases

The Creator plan's entry price went from $15/month to $33/month (annual billing) for 1,000 subscribers — a 120% increase at the entry tier. Multiple Trustpilot reviewers report prices effectively tripling mid-contract for larger lists. The free Newsletter plan's 10,000-subscriber limit was untouched, which is the one piece of good news.

One more thing worth knowing: Kit's legacy convertkit.com Trustpilot profile — accumulated before the rebrand — sits at just 1.9★, while the new kit.com profile shows 4.2★. If you're comparing review scores across sources, make sure you're looking at the right profile, since older reviews and newer ones tell very different stories.

Common questions

Kit FAQs

Is Kit still worth it after the price hike?
Yes for the free plan — 10,000 subscribers at no cost is genuinely unmatched. The paid tiers are a harder call after the September 2025 increase; they're worth it if you specifically want tag-based automation and built-in monetization, but shop around if you're purely price-sensitive.
Why did ConvertKit rebrand to Kit?
ConvertKit rebranded to Kit in 2024 to broaden its positioning beyond writers to any online creator or small business with a creator-shaped list. The underlying product — tag-based automation, Commerce, the free-tier structure — stayed the same.
How big was the Kit price increase?
The Creator plan's entry price rose from $15/month to $33/month (annual billing) in September 2025 — a 120% increase at the 1,000-subscriber tier. Some legacy customers on larger lists reported increases of 100-400%.
Kit vs ActiveCampaign — which is better?
Kit is simpler and cheaper for creators who mainly need newsletters and light automation; ActiveCampaign has meaningfully deeper automation and a built-in CRM for more complex sales workflows. If your business is content and audience-first, Kit usually wins; if it's sales-process-first, ActiveCampaign does. See our full comparison.
Is Kit's free plan actually usable long-term?
Yes — unlike most "free trials," Kit's Newsletter plan is a genuine permanent tier supporting up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends. The main limitation is just one automation, so it works well until you need real workflow logic.
Why does Kit have two different Trustpilot ratings?
The legacy convertkit.com profile — built up before the 2024 rebrand — shows 1.9★, while the new kit.com profile shows 4.2★. Both are the same company; make sure you know which profile you're looking at before trusting a Trustpilot score for Kit.
