Our verdict

Both call themselves "all-in-one," but they mean different things by it. GetResponse's version of all-in-one is breadth — webinars, funnels, landing pages and email bundled together at a fair price, with a real free plan to start on. ActiveCampaign's version is depth — the most capable automation and CRM combination in this category, at the cost of no free plan and a steeper learning curve.

If your marketing revolves around content and events — webinars, launches, newsletters — GetResponse's bundle is hard to beat for the price. If you're managing a genuine sales process with multiple stages and need branching logic that reacts to specific customer behavior, ActiveCampaign's depth justifies the higher floor. Match the platform to the complexity of what you're actually running, not just the "all-in-one" label both use.

Common questions

GetResponse vs ActiveCampaign FAQs

Is GetResponse or ActiveCampaign better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different jobs. GetResponse is better if you want webinars, funnels and a real free plan; ActiveCampaign is better if you need a built-in CRM and genuinely complex, branching automation.
Which is cheaper?
ActiveCampaign's Starter plan ($15/mo) slightly undercuts GetResponse's Starter ($19/mo), but ActiveCampaign has no free plan at all — GetResponse's 500-contact free tier means it can be genuinely free for longer. ActiveCampaign's CRM also sits behind the pricier Plus plan ($49/mo).
Does GetResponse have a CRM?
No — GetResponse is focused on email, automation, webinars and funnels, with no deal pipeline or lead-scoring functionality. If you need a CRM, ActiveCampaign (Plus plan and up) or a dedicated CRM tool is the better fit.
Does ActiveCampaign offer webinars?
No — ActiveCampaign has no native webinar hosting at any price tier. GetResponse includes it from the Marketer plan ($59/mo) up, a genuine differentiator if webinars are part of your marketing.
Can I switch from GetResponse to ActiveCampaign later?
Yes, though migrating contacts, tags and automation logic takes manual work on both platforms — there's no official one-click migration tool between them.
