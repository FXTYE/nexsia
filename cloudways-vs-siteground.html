Our verdict

Both hosts genuinely lean into cloud infrastructure, just with very different philosophies. Cloudways gives you raw flexibility — five cloud providers, usage-based pricing, and no renewal shock — in exchange for managing more of the technical setup yourself. SiteGround gives you a single, polished Google Cloud experience with the best support reputation in this category, at a real premium and with a genuine renewal jump.

If you're technical, price-conscious about renewals specifically, or want to compare cloud providers, Cloudways is the structurally better fit. If you want phone support and a managed panel without touching server configuration, SiteGround's premium is worth paying — just budget honestly for its renewal rate, which varies by your original term length.

Common questions

Cloudways vs SiteGround FAQs

Is Cloudways or SiteGround better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different comfort levels. Cloudways is better if you're technical and want no renewal shock; SiteGround is better if you want phone support and a managed, polished panel without touching server configuration.
Which is cheaper?
SiteGround's intro price ($2.99/mo) is lower than Cloudways' entry tier ($11/mo), but SiteGround's renewal jumps 400-500%+ depending on your original term. Cloudways has no equivalent jump — over a few years, the total cost gap narrows significantly depending on your usage.
Do both hosts use Google Cloud?
SiteGround runs exclusively on Google Cloud. Cloudways offers Google Cloud as one of five provider options — you can also choose DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, or AWS depending on your needs and budget.
Does Cloudways offer phone support?
Not on standard plans — Cloudways offers 24/7 live chat, with phone-equivalent access to senior engineers available on Pro-tier plans. SiteGround includes phone support as standard across its plans.
Can I host multiple websites on SiteGround's entry plan?
No — StartUp is capped at a single website. Cloudways doesn't have this limitation; you can host as many sites as your server resources support, scaling as needed.
