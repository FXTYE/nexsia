Our verdict

These two serve genuinely different stages of the same journey. Mailchimp is the easiest on-ramp to email marketing — the template library and drag-and-drop editor mean you can send something professional-looking on day one, and the free tier (while shrinking) still exists.

ActiveCampaign is what most businesses eventually need once broadcast emails stop being enough — branching automation and a built-in CRM justify the steeper learning curve and higher price once your marketing needs a real sales process behind it. If you're not sure which stage you're at, start with what you need today: Mailchimp if you're mailing a simple newsletter, ActiveCampaign if you're already thinking in terms of pipelines and triggers.

Common questions

Mailchimp vs ActiveCampaign FAQs

Is Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different jobs. Mailchimp is better for beginners who want the easiest learning curve and best templates; ActiveCampaign is better if you need a built-in CRM and genuinely complex, branching automation.
Which is cheaper?
Mailchimp's free plan (250 contacts) beats anything ActiveCampaign offers, since ActiveCampaign has no free tier at all. On paid plans the entry prices are close ($13/mo vs $15/mo), but ActiveCampaign's CRM sits behind the pricier Plus plan ($49/mo) — Mailchimp's core features are available at its lowest paid tier.
Does Mailchimp have a CRM?
No — Mailchimp is focused on email campaigns, templates and basic automation, with no deal pipeline or lead-scoring functionality. If you need a CRM, ActiveCampaign (Plus plan and up) or a dedicated CRM tool is the better fit.
Why did ActiveCampaign's billing change in November 2025?
As of November 3, 2025, new ActiveCampaign accounts are billed for every contact regardless of status, including unsubscribed and bounced ones — not just active contacts as before. Accounts created earlier keep the old pricing structure.
Can I switch from Mailchimp to ActiveCampaign later?
Yes, though migrating contacts, tags and automation logic takes manual work on both platforms — there's no official one-click migration tool between them.
