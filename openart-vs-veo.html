Our verdict

For most people, OpenArt is the more useful subscription. It includes access to Veo-class video generation alongside 100+ image models and a genuinely effective character-consistency tool, usually at a lower cost per generation than paying Google directly — without giving up much on quality, since it's routing to the same underlying models.

Veo 3 still earns its place if native, built-in dialogue and sound effects are the single most important thing to your project, or you're already fully inside the Gemini ecosystem and don't need image generation at all.

Common questions

OpenArt vs Veo 3 FAQs

Is OpenArt better than Veo 3?
For most people, yes — OpenArt gives you image generation, video (including access to Veo itself) and character consistency in one workspace, usually cheaper per generation. Veo 3 wins specifically on native, built-in audio generation and narrow photorealism.
Can I actually generate Veo-quality video inside OpenArt?
Yes — OpenArt Wonder routes video requests to underlying models including Veo, Kling and Seedance, so you get access to Veo-class output without a separate Google AI subscription.
Which is cheaper?
OpenArt, on both the subscription (from $7/mo vs $19.99/mo) and typically on cost per generation, since it shops the same request across providers rather than being locked to one.
Does OpenArt generate audio like Veo 3 does?
It depends on the underlying model you select for a generation. Veo 3's native, synced dialogue and sound-effect generation remains its standout, unmatched feature.
Which should I pick if I only need video, not images?
If you specifically want Veo's native audio and are fully inside the Gemini ecosystem, go direct to Veo 3. If you want flexibility across multiple video models plus the option of image generation later, OpenArt is the safer long-term pick.
