Our verdict

These two solve overlapping but genuinely different problems. GetResponse's real advantage is breadth — webinars, funnels and unlimited sends bundled into a fair entry price mean you're less likely to outgrow it and need a second tool. Mailchimp's advantage is polish — the best template library and easiest learning curve in the category, if you don't need what GetResponse bundles in.

The honest caveat on both: read the negative reviews before committing annually. Mailchimp's Trustpilot score (2.9★) is more uniformly negative around billing and support; GetResponse's (4.2★) is bimodal, meaning plenty of people are genuinely happy — but the unhappy 27% describe real cancellation and billing friction too. Test on monthly billing first if you're unsure.

Common questions

GetResponse vs Mailchimp FAQs

Is GetResponse or Mailchimp better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different jobs. GetResponse is better if you want webinars, funnels and unlimited sends bundled in; Mailchimp is better if you want the easiest possible learning curve and the biggest template library.
Which is cheaper?
Mailchimp's entry price is slightly lower ($13/mo vs $19/mo), but Mailchimp caps sends at roughly 12x your contact count while GetResponse includes unlimited sends on every paid plan — high-frequency senders often find GetResponse cheaper in practice.
Does Mailchimp offer webinars?
No — Mailchimp has no webinar hosting at any price tier. GetResponse includes native webinar hosting from the Marketer plan ($59/mo) up, a genuine differentiator if webinars are part of your marketing.
Why does GetResponse have mixed reviews?
GetResponse's Trustpilot rating (4.2★) is bimodal — 63% five-star, 27% one-star — mostly driven by billing disputes and cancellation difficulty. Mailchimp's rating (2.9★) is more uniformly negative for similar reasons. Both are worth reading carefully before committing to annual billing.
Can I switch from Mailchimp to GetResponse later?
Yes, though migrating contacts, tags and automation logic takes manual work on both platforms — there's no official one-click migration tool between them.
