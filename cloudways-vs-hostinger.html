Our verdict

These two aren't really competing for the same buyer. Cloudways solves a real, structural problem — the renewal-shock pricing that defines nearly every host in this category — by charging for what you actually use, with genuine cloud infrastructure flexibility. Hostinger solves a different problem: getting a first website online as cheaply and simply as possible, with email hosting and a familiar control panel included.

If you're technical, running an agency, or specifically want to avoid ever being surprised by a renewal bill, Cloudways is the structurally better choice. Common questions

Cloudways vs Hostinger FAQs

Is Cloudways or Hostinger better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different users. Cloudways is better if you're technical and want no renewal shock; Hostinger is better if you're a first-time site owner who wants the cheapest, simplest way to get online.
Which is cheaper?
Hostinger's intro price ($2.69/mo) is lower than Cloudways' entry tier ($11/mo), but that requires a 48-month prepaid commitment. Cloudways has no equivalent renewal jump — over several years, the total cost can end up closer than the intro prices suggest, depending on your usage.
Does Cloudways include cPanel?
No — Cloudways uses its own custom dashboard, not cPanel. Hostinger's hPanel is closer to a traditional cPanel-style experience, which is why Hostinger is generally considered more beginner-friendly.
Does Cloudways offer email hosting?
No — Cloudways doesn't include email hosting at any tier. Hostinger includes it standard, which is a real practical advantage if you need a business email address alongside your website.
Can I switch from Hostinger to Cloudways later?
Yes — Cloudways offers 1 free migration for new customers. Additional migrations for other applications start at $25 each. The process requires more technical comfort than Hostinger's more guided migration tools.
