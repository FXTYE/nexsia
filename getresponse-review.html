The quick verdict

GetResponse genuinely earns its "all-in-one" label — email, marketing automation, unlimited landing pages, and native webinar hosting all live under one login at a fair $19/month entry price, and G2's 1,100+ reviews consistently praise the ease of use.

But the review pattern here is unusually split. Unlike most tools where Trustpilot skews low across the board, GetResponse's Trustpilot rating (4.2★) is bimodal — 63% five-star, but a real 27% one-star, mostly billing disputes, difficult cancellation, and surprise charges after a "free" trial. If you're comfortable reading the fine print and testing on a monthly plan first, the product itself delivers real value — just don't commit annually until you've confirmed it fits.

Know before you buy

The bimodal Trustpilot split, honestly

This is the detail most reviews smooth over by just quoting the average. GetResponse's 4.2★ Trustpilot rating looks solid on the surface, but the actual distribution is unusually split: 63% five-star reviews, and a real 27% one-star — not a smooth curve, a genuine love-it-or-hate-it pattern.

Billing and cancellation complaints are consistent across independent sources

Reviewers across Trustpilot, BBB and Sitejabber describe difficulty cancelling, surprise charges after a "free" trial, and being billed for a website builder they thought was free. If there's any chance you might switch platforms, start with monthly billing and test for at least one full billing cycle before committing annually — the discount isn't worth the risk if the platform isn't right for you.

Two more things worth knowing: the Starter plan ($19/mo) includes only 1 automation workflow and no webinar hosting — both require upgrading to Marketer ($59/mo) or higher. And bounced or removed contacts still count toward your subscriber limit until you manually clean your list, which can quietly push you into a higher tier.

Common questions

GetResponse FAQs

Is GetResponse worth it in 2026?
Yes, if you want email, automation, landing pages and webinars genuinely bundled under one login at a fair entry price. Go in aware of the bimodal Trustpilot reviews — test on a monthly plan first if there's any chance you'll want to cancel.
Why does GetResponse have such mixed reviews?
GetResponse's Trustpilot rating (4.2★) is bimodal — 63% five-star and 27% one-star, not a smooth distribution. The negative reviews cluster heavily around billing, cancellation difficulty, and surprise charges after a free trial, while the product itself is consistently praised on G2 and Capterra.
What's actually included in the Starter plan?
The $19/month Starter plan includes unlimited email sends, an AI email generator, autoresponders, and a website builder — but only 1 automation workflow and no webinar hosting. Both require upgrading to Marketer ($59/mo) or higher.
GetResponse vs ActiveCampaign — which is better?
ActiveCampaign has deeper, more flexible automation and a dedicated CRM; GetResponse is broader — it adds native webinars, a website builder and course monetization that ActiveCampaign doesn't offer. If automation depth is your priority, ActiveCampaign wins; if breadth of built-in tools matters more, GetResponse wins.
Does GetResponse have a free plan?
Yes — the Free plan supports up to 500 contacts with basic email tools and a website builder, though it has no automation workflows. There's also a 30-day trial of premium features for new signups.
Do bounced contacts count toward my limit?
Yes — unlike some competitors, GetResponse doesn't automatically remove bounced or unsubscribed contacts from your count. You'll need to clean your list manually to avoid being pushed into a more expensive tier as your bounce rate accumulates.
