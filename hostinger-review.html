The quick verdict

Hostinger genuinely earns its reputation as the budget champion — the intro pricing is real, the hPanel dashboard is faster to learn than traditional cPanel, and the 4.7★ Trustpilot score across 64,000+ reviews is the largest, most consistent sample in this category.

The honest caveat, and it's a real one: that headline $2.69/month price only exists on a 48-month term paid upfront — $95.52 due at signup — and the renewal jumps to $10.99/month, a 268-550% increase depending on which plan you started on. None of this makes Hostinger bad hosting; it makes the pricing page misleading if you don't read past the big number. Budget for the renewal rate from day one, not the intro rate, and Hostinger remains a genuinely strong pick.

Know before you buy

The 48-month lock-in, honestly

This is the detail that catches more people than any other complaint about Hostinger. The eye-catching $1.99-2.69/month price you see in ads and comparison charts is real — but it only applies if you commit to and prepay the full 48-month term at checkout, which for Premium comes to $95.52 due today, not spread out.

Renewal is 268-550% higher than the intro price

Once your term ends, Premium renews at $10.99/month and Business at $16.99/month — regardless of which term length you originally chose. A shorter 12-month term gets you the same renewal rate but a higher intro price, so the 48-month plan is genuinely the best value if you're confident you'll stay — the risk is locking in for 4 years on a site you might abandon in 6 months.

Two more things worth knowing: there's no phone support at any tier — you're limited to live chat and tickets. And the 30-day money-back guarantee is real and honored, but it only covers your first term; if you're on a 48-month plan and want out in month 13, you're not getting a refund on months 2-48.

Common questions

Hostinger FAQs

Is Hostinger actually as cheap as advertised?
The intro price is real, but only on the 48-month term paid upfront — for Premium that's $95.52 due at signup, not a small monthly charge. Shorter terms cost more per month. Budget for the renewal rate ($10.99/mo for Premium), not the intro rate, when deciding if it fits your budget.
Why does Hostinger's price jump so much at renewal?
This is standard practice across the budget hosting industry, not unique to Hostinger — the intro discount only applies to your first term. Hostinger's jump (268-550% depending on plan) is on the higher end but comparable to SiteGround's 400-500% jumps and Bluehost's similar pattern.
Does Hostinger have phone support?
No — Hostinger offers live chat and support tickets only, at every plan tier. If phone support matters to you, SiteGround and Bluehost both offer it on at least some plans.
Hostinger vs SiteGround — which is better?
Hostinger wins on price and raw speed; SiteGround wins on support quality and managed WordPress features. If you're price-sensitive and comfortable managing things yourself, Hostinger is the better value. If support and hand-holding matter more, SiteGround is worth the higher price. See our full comparison.
Can I cancel and get a refund after the 48-month term?
The 30-day money-back guarantee only covers your first 30 days on a new term. If you're on a 48-month plan and want to cancel in month 13, you won't get a refund for the remaining months — this is a real commitment, not a rolling subscription you can cancel anytime.
Is Hostinger good for WordPress?
Yes — 1-click WordPress installation, staging environments on higher tiers, and Kodee AI assistance for setup and troubleshooting. It's not as WordPress-specialized as SiteGround's managed hosting, but it's genuinely capable for most WordPress sites.
