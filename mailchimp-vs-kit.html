Our verdict

These two increasingly serve different audiences, not just different budgets. Mailchimp remains the generalist's choice — the best templates, the broadest recognition, and a familiar tool for teams and agencies who don't need creator-specific features. Kit has doubled down on being the creator's platform, with monetization tools and a free tier built around the reality that most creators start with zero revenue and grow from there.

If your business is genuinely about content — a newsletter, a course, a podcast — Kit's free tier and native Commerce tools solve real problems Mailchimp doesn't attempt. If you're running general marketing email for a business that isn't creator-led, Mailchimp's design tools and familiarity still make sense. The "which is cheaper" question matters less than "which audience are you actually serving."

Common questions

Mailchimp vs Kit FAQs

Is Mailchimp or Kit better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different audiences. Mailchimp is better for general businesses that want the biggest template library; Kit is better if you're a creator who wants a generous free tier and native tools to sell digital products.
Which is cheaper?
Mailchimp's entry price is lower ($13/mo vs $33/mo), but Kit's free plan (10,000 subscribers) is far more generous than Mailchimp's (250 contacts). If you're under 10,000 subscribers, Kit can be genuinely free for longer than Mailchimp.
Can I sell products through Mailchimp?
Not natively for digital products — Mailchimp is focused on email campaigns and has ecommerce integrations for physical-product stores like Shopify, but no built-in way to sell a course or paid newsletter the way Kit's Commerce feature does.
Why did Kit's prices go up so much?
Kit rebranded from ConvertKit in 2024 and raised prices roughly 120% at the entry tier in September 2025 — Creator went from $15 to $33/month. The free Newsletter plan's 10,000-subscriber limit was untouched.
Can I switch from Mailchimp to Kit later?
Yes, though migrating contacts, tags and automation logic takes manual work on both platforms — there's no official one-click migration tool between them.
