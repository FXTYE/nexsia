The quick verdict

Mailchimp is still the most approachable way to start with email marketing — the drag-and-drop editor, template library, and basic automation are genuinely easy to pick up, and G2 reviewers consistently rate the day-to-day product highly.

But the value calculation has shifted since Intuit's 2021 acquisition. The free plan was cut from 500 to 250 contacts in February 2026, pricing climbs quickly once you're on a paid tier, and support drops to nothing after your first 30 days on the free plan. If your business already runs on a funnel platform, check whether it includes email marketing natively before paying for a separate tool — Mailchimp is the right pick when you specifically need best-in-class templates and don't mind the price climb as your list grows.

Know before you buy

The shrinking free plan, honestly

This is the detail that catches a lot of people off guard, because Mailchimp doesn't advertise it loudly. In February 2026, the free plan's contact limit was cut from 500 to 250, continuing a pattern that's been running since Intuit's 2021 acquisition — the free plan supported 2,000 contacts as recently as 2022.

The free plan is closer to a trial than a permanent option

250 contacts, 500 sends a month, no scheduling, no multi-step automation, Mailchimp branding on every email, and support only for your first 30 days. It works for testing the platform — it's not really enough to run an ongoing newsletter for most small businesses.

Two more costs worth budgeting for: pricing is based on total contact count, including unsubscribed contacts still sitting in your account, so an unclean list can quietly push you into a higher tier. And Essentials — the cheapest paid plan — locks out multi-step automations and dynamic content entirely; you need Standard for those, which roughly doubles the price at most list sizes.

Common questions

Mailchimp FAQs

Is Mailchimp still worth it in 2026?
Yes, if you're just starting out and want the easiest possible learning curve, or you have a small list and value the template library. It's a harder sell once you need real automation or your list grows past a few thousand contacts, since competitors like ActiveCampaign and Kit offer more automation depth for a comparable or lower price at scale.
Why did Mailchimp's free plan get worse?
Mailchimp has cut the free plan's limits almost every year since Intuit's 2021 acquisition — from 2,000 contacts in 2022, to 500 in 2023, to just 250 as of February 2026. Automation was fully removed from the free tier by mid-2025.
Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?
Yes — pricing is based on total contact count, including unsubscribed and inactive contacts still stored in your account. Regularly cleaning your list can help avoid being pushed into a higher tier unnecessarily.
Mailchimp vs ActiveCampaign — which is better?
Mailchimp is easier to learn and has a better template library; ActiveCampaign has meaningfully deeper automation and a built-in CRM. If you're past basic broadcast emails and need real workflow automation, ActiveCampaign is usually the better long-term fit. See our full comparison.
Why is Mailchimp's Trustpilot rating so much lower than G2?
G2 and Capterra reviewers tend to evaluate the product itself, where Mailchimp scores well (4.3-4.4/5). Trustpilot skews toward people who had a support or billing problem, which is where Mailchimp scores poorly (2.9/5, with 67% one-star reviews) — mostly centered on account access and cancellation issues rather than the email tools themselves.
Is there a genuinely free version of Mailchimp?
Yes, but it's limited — 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with no scheduling or automation, and support only for your first 30 days. It's enough to test the platform, not to run an ongoing newsletter for most small businesses.
