The quick verdict

Bluehost's real strength is legitimacy, not polish — it's one of the few hosts WordPress.org actually recommends by name, and the entry-level renewal price (~$9.99/mo) is genuinely the cheapest of the three major hosts compared here, even beating Hostinger's renewal rate.

The honest caveat: Bluehost's Trustpilot score (3.2★) sits meaningfully below both Hostinger and SiteGround, and the complaints aren't vague — reviewers consistently describe aggressive checkout upsells, domain redemption fees that feel punitive, and support quality that varies more than its two main competitors. If the WordPress.org endorsement and the low renewal price matter to you, Bluehost is a defensible pick — just read the checkout screen carefully and skip add-ons you don't need.

Know before you buy

The Trustpilot gap, honestly

This is the detail that separates Bluehost from its two main competitors most clearly. At 3.2★, Bluehost's Trustpilot score sits well below Hostinger's 4.7★ and SiteGround's 4.7★ — and the gap isn't noise, it reflects a real, consistent pattern of complaints across a 29,000+ review sample.

Domain redemption fees and checkout upsells are the most-cited complaints

Multiple reviewers describe being charged unexpectedly high fees to "redeem" a domain when cancelling — sometimes over £100 for something that costs a few dollars elsewhere. Checkout is also more upsell-heavy than Hostinger or SiteGround, with several optional add-ons pre-selected by default. Read the checkout screen carefully and deselect anything you don't specifically want.

One thing genuinely in Bluehost's favor: its renewal price is the lowest of the three hosts compared here — Basic renews around $8.99-9.99/month, actually cheaper than Hostinger's Premium renewal ($10.99/mo) and far below SiteGround's StartUp renewal. If you go in aware of the checkout upsells and budget for the (comparatively modest) renewal jump, the underlying economics are genuinely competitive.

Common questions

Bluehost FAQs

Is Bluehost actually recommended by WordPress.org?
Yes — Bluehost is one of a small number of hosts WordPress.org names directly as a recommended provider, a genuine credential rather than a marketing claim. This doesn't mean it's the best host on every metric, but it is a real, verifiable endorsement.
Why is Bluehost's Trustpilot score so much lower than Hostinger or SiteGround?
The pattern across reviews points to domain redemption fees, checkout upsells, and inconsistent support quality as the main drivers. It's a real, well-documented gap (3.2★ vs 4.7★ for both competitors) across a large review sample, not a handful of outlier complaints.
What is a domain redemption fee?
If you registered a free domain through Bluehost and later want to cancel your hosting while keeping the domain, some reviewers report being charged a high fee to "redeem" it — sometimes over £100. Verify the current policy before you commit if this matters to you.
Bluehost vs Hostinger — which is better?
Bluehost has the official WordPress.org endorsement and a slightly lower renewal price; Hostinger has a dramatically better Trustpilot track record and generally faster benchmarked performance. For most people, Hostinger's review consistency makes it the safer recommendation. See our full comparison.
Does Bluehost have hidden fees?
Not hidden exactly, but checkout includes more pre-selected optional add-ons than competitors, and domain redemption fees are a documented complaint. Review your cart carefully before completing purchase, and check the renewal price for your specific plan in advance.
Is Bluehost good for beginners?
Yes, functionally — 1-click WordPress install and AI-assisted setup make it accessible for first-time site owners. The caveat is support consistency; some beginners report excellent help, others report frustrating experiences, so set expectations accordingly.
