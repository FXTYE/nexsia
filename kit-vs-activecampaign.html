Our verdict

These two are built for different jobs more than they compete directly. Kit is a content and audience platform that happens to include light automation and monetization; ActiveCampaign is a sales and marketing automation platform that happens to send good-looking emails. If your business runs on content — a newsletter, a course, digital products — Kit's free tier and simplicity are hard to beat.

But if you're managing a sales process with multiple stages, or you need automation logic beyond simple tag-based sequences, ActiveCampaign's CRM and branching workflows are worth the higher price and the extra account-management care the November 2025 billing change now requires. Match the platform to what your business actually does, not just which one is cheaper to start.

Common questions

Kit vs ActiveCampaign FAQs

Is Kit or ActiveCampaign better?
Neither is 'better' outright — they're built for different jobs. Kit is better for content-first creators who want a free tier and simplicity; ActiveCampaign is better if you need a built-in CRM and genuinely complex, branching automation.
Which is cheaper?
Kit's free plan (10,000 subscribers) beats anything ActiveCampaign offers, since ActiveCampaign has no free tier at all. On paid plans, ActiveCampaign's $15/mo Starter undercuts Kit's $33/mo Creator plan — but Starter lacks the CRM that's the real reason to choose ActiveCampaign in the first place.
Does Kit have a CRM?
No — Kit is focused on email, automation and content monetization, with no deal pipeline or lead-scoring functionality. If you need a CRM, ActiveCampaign (Plus plan and up) or a dedicated CRM tool is the better fit.
Why did ActiveCampaign's billing change in November 2025?
As of November 3, 2025, new ActiveCampaign accounts are billed for every contact regardless of status, including unsubscribed and bounced ones — not just active contacts as before. Accounts created earlier keep the old pricing structure.
Can I switch from Kit to ActiveCampaign later?
Yes, though migrating subscribers, tags and automation logic takes manual work on both platforms — there's no official one-click migration tool between them.
